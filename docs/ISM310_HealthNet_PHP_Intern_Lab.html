Base case: Health Network, Inc. is the fictional health-services company in the course. HNetExchange handles medical messages, HNetPay supports payment and billing, and HNetConnect provides a directory. Third parties manage production hosting.

Exercise additions: E1–E6 below introduce PHP exposure, provider tickets, alerts, and recovery results. They are invented teaching records, not findings about a real organization or extra facts in your project source document.

  1. Form a team of 3–4. Rotate intern analyst, evidence checker, manager, and reporter.
  2. Read each evidence record. Choose a decision and enter the requested short responses in your own words.
  3. Select “Check and record this step,” read the feedback, then use Continue. Later steps unlock in order. Incorrect decisions can still proceed after review because this is practice.
  4. Finish all eight steps, review the self-checklist, and download the intern packet. The game checks completion, not the quality of your written analysis.
  5. Report your risk statement, priority rationale, treatment recommendation, and one unresolved question. Use the workbook if the browser is unavailable.

Allow 35 minutes for the lab and 15 minutes for discussion. No live scanning or exploit testing is involved. Do not enter names, credentials, personal data, or graded submission text. Responses save on this browser when storage is available. No account or server submission is used.

Public facilitator answers

1. Prepare the assessment

Define the question, owners, scope, evidence needs, and authorized assessment methods.

Prepare a scoped inquiry with the responsible staff. An advisory does not establish Health Network exposure or compromise. The intern collects and analyzes authorized information; a manager owns decisions.

2. Identify assets and exposure

Prioritize the confirmed HNetPay runtime path and verify the other product dependencies separately.

E2 establishes a vulnerability and exposure path for the exercise HNetPay service. Record the provider, owner, runtime evidence, and unknowns. Do not extend that finding to every product or call it a breach.

3. Write the risk statement

An unauthenticated attacker could exploit the affected HNetPay runtime, potentially exposing information or interrupting payment operations.

A useful risk statement links threat event, weakness, asset or process, and potential consequence. E3 is an investigation lead with an alternative explanation. It does not prove exploitation or disclosure.

4. Assess likelihood and impact

State the rating, evidence, assumptions, and uncertainty; use it for prioritization, not proof of compromise.

A defensible reference answer is likelihood 3 and impact 3, giving High (9), if the assumed major payment/data impact is explained. Other impact judgments need evidence and scope reasoning. A high priority does not establish a breach, loss estimate, or statistical probability.

5. Check compliance relevance

Ask for payment data-flow and contractual evidence, confirm HIPAA role where relevant, and state that CUI applicability is unestablished.

Distinguish a standard from a law and record triggering facts. PCI DSS, HIPAA, and SP 800-171 have different scopes. The intern asks the compliance lead to validate applicability rather than inventing a compliance certification.

6. Recommend risk treatment

Recommend the applicable update with staged validation; request approval for an interim restriction and continuity arrangements; assign owners and review criteria.

The provider implements the authorized runtime change. An interim restriction reduces exposure while affecting service. Least privilege, monitoring, recovery, and governance serve different roles. Management decides and documents treatment or time-limited acceptance; outsourcing does not erase accountability.

7. Verify and communicate

Request active-runtime and business verification, preserve the investigation trail, and report residual uncertainties to the manager.

Specify evidence of the running fix, safe validation, service tests, and any authorized incident review. Reject AI certainty without evidence. A remediated flaw and a historical exposure investigation are separate findings. Residual risk stays provisional until control effectiveness and scope are reviewed.

8. Maintain and review

RTO missed, RPO met, MTD unknown; reconcile pending work and schedule review after changes or new evidence.

Elapsed service recovery and age of recovered data are different measures. The business owner validates usable payment operations and reconciliation. Reassess after material changes, new threat evidence, control failures, or an agreed review interval. Monitoring maintains the assessment.
